The problem

Three records. None of them wrong. All of them different.

Every settlement cycle leaves a small gap nobody has time to chase. Individually invisible, annually expensive.

Money quietly pulled back

A customer disputes a payment weeks later. The amount, plus a fee, disappears from a settlement with nothing telling you which one.

Fees above your rate card

Half a basis point over contract, across a thousand transactions. Your settlement and your bank agree perfectly — and both are wrong.

Money held, not lost

A rolling reserve is withheld this month and returned two months later. Looked at one month at a time, it looks like a loss and then a windfall.

How it works

It tries four ways to explain a gap
before it ever asks you

Each step only looks at what the step before it could not resolve. Anything left over is called unexplained, never guessed at.

Step 1

Match on the reference number

The settlement's UTR against the bank's reference, amounts tying exactly.

Step 2

Check the settlement's own arithmetic

Payments, minus refunds, minus disputes — does the settlement add up on its own terms, regardless of what the bank says?

Step 3

Allow for banks renaming things

A rewritten reference or a truncated description is noise, not a loss. Amounts are never matched approximately — money either ties or it is a real gap.

Step 4

Investigate what is left, with AI

Only the leftovers reach a model, and only to suggest a cause. Its suggestion is re-checked from your raw records before anyone acts on it.

Then

Prove the books balance, and record the decision

Every rupee collected is matched against every door it could have left through. The result is written to a permanent log that is added to, never edited.

Why you can trust it

The AI is never allowed to decide

You can make the AI actively dishonest inside the app and watch what stops it. Four different attacks, four different defences, nothing reaching your books.

What the AI tries Applied to your books What stopped it
Invents evidence that does not exist None Checked against your real records first
Understates an amount to slip under the approval limit None The amount is recalculated, never taken on trust
Blames something real but unrelated None The arithmetic simply fails to add up
Claims total confidence with nothing behind it None Evidence is checked before confidence is read

The one worth seeing

The AI cites a genuine dispute and claims the correction is worth ₹0.01 when it is really worth ₹2,463.28 — small enough to be approved automatically with nobody watching. It fails, because the part of the system that decides works the number out itself instead of asking the AI.

Compared against doing it the simple way

The same data also runs through plain reference matching — what most reconciliation scripts actually are. On a renamed bank reference it reports money missing that had arrived. On overcharged fees it reports everything fine while ₹259.69 walks out the door.

Every number traceable to a run you started

The app ships with no data in it. No sample figures, no seeded merchant. You pick a scenario, watch it execute, and every screen fills with that run's real results.

What it does not do

The limits, before you find them

A reconciliation tool that oversells itself is the exact thing this was built against.

The live Razorpay connection is untested

The API client, the data mapping and the whole pipeline are built and tested against recorded responses. The one piece that makes the actual network call has never run against a real account, because no keys were available. The app says so on screen rather than letting you discover it.

Bank statements are read, not uploaded

A statement is picked up from a file rather than dragged into the browser. Storage is in-memory rather than a database, and there is one merchant rather than real multi-tenancy.

Each of these is a known boundary with a known shape — written down, not discovered later.
