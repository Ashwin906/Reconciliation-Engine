Problem taste

Three records. Razorpay's dashboard, the bank statement, and the merchant's own books. Each one internally consistent. None of them agree.

A chargeback pulled back without notice. A settlement that never landed. A fee half a basis point over contract, across a thousand transactions. Individually invisible. Annually, it is real money.

The bank renamed a reference number. Exact matching fails outright — and the fallback tiers recover it. 100% reconciled, books balance to zero.

Now the same data through plain reference matching, which is what most reconciliation scripts actually are. Eighty percent. It reports money missing that actually arrived — and that is the expensive kind of wrong. It sends someone hunting for a payment that is already in the bank.

Four hundred and seventy-six tests. Money is integer paise, never a float. And the layer that narrates this computes nothing — it reads the pipeline's output, so the demo cannot drift from what the system actually does.

AI judgment
Click “You were overcharged fees”. Land on the verdict. Point at the warning line: books balanced, money still gone. Then switch the left rail to Attack it. Do not run one yet.

Where does AI belong in this? Almost nowhere.

Four deterministic tiers run before a model is ever consulted. Take this one: it reconciles at 100%. Settlement and bank agree exactly. And ₹259.69 still walked out the door — because the overcharge happened before the settlement was calculated. Both numbers are internally consistent and both are wrong.

Rule-based detection catches that. No model involved. Arithmetic does not need one.

The model is used for exactly one thing: proposing a cause for what is left over. It never decides. And you don't have to take my word for that — you can make it lie.

3:0075 sec
Failure recovery
Click “Understate the amount”. Wait for the green panel. Point at the claimed vs actual rows, then the badge row. Optional cut to FAILURES.md, failure #1.

Here is the attack that matters. The model cites a real dispute and claims the correction is worth one paisa — when it is actually ₹2,463.28. Small enough to slip under the auto-approve limit and be written to the books with nobody watching. It states 99% confidence.

Zero applied automatically. The gate recomputes the amount from raw data. It never reads the model's number.

I know that attack works because we shipped that bug. The gate used to trust the model's claimed amount. That is failure number one in a log of thirteen, each written down with what broke and how it was caught.

My favourite is number thirteen. Our own reserve demo produced a table where every individual number was correct — supporting a conclusion that was false. That is far harder to catch than a wrong number, and it nearly shipped as the headline proof.

Build quality · honesty
Cut to landing.html → “The limits, before you find them”. End on the hero, or on the Activity log showing real rows.

What does not work. The client that actually calls Razorpay's API has never run against live keys — we had none. Everything either side of it is tested against recorded payloads, and the app says so on screen rather than letting you find out. Storage is in memory, not Postgres. One merchant, not real multi-tenancy.

Those are boundaries we wrote down, not discoveries waiting for you.

The claim is not that this system is never wrong. It is that when it is wrong, it says so — and nothing it cannot prove is ever allowed to touch someone's books.

Do not say “zero modifications to the ledger.”

An adversarial run does write ledger rows — gate=HUMAN, status=PENDING_APPROVAL — on purpose. If a judge opens the Activity log after your demo and sees rows, an overstated claim costs you the whole segment. The true and stronger line is “zero corrections applied automatically.”

Do not merge the four attacks into one defence.

Fabricated evidence is stopped before the gate. The understated amount reaches the gate and is caught by independent recomputation. Saying one defence catches everything is both wrong and a weaker story than “four attacks, four different defences.”

How to actually record this

It is a recorded video, so nothing has to be performed in one take. Use that.

  1. Record the screen and the voice separately. Capture clean screen footage of each beat first, with no talking, then read the script over it. You stop fighting to click and speak in rhythm at the same time, and a fluffed line costs one retake of audio instead of the whole run.
  2. Record the five beats as five clips. Any mistake costs you one beat, never the whole video. Cut them together in order.
  3. Before capturing: start both servers, then click Clear all so the app is genuinely empty. The empty state is part of the argument — it proves nothing is seeded.
  4. Confirm the app opens on Watch it happen, not Overview.
  5. Each scenario animation runs about four seconds. Capture it once, then trim it to fit the narration rather than padding your speech to match it.
  6. Browser at 100% zoom. Pick light or dark and stay there. Hide bookmarks, close other tabs, silence notifications.
  7. Record at 1080p minimum — the rupee figures are the whole point and they must be legible after compression.
  8. Do not add background music under the numbers. Let ₹0.01 against ₹2,463.28 land in silence.
